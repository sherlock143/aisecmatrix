What "Password Breached" Actually Means (And What to Do)

If you've ever run a password through a breach checker and seen "found 4,213 times," it's a jarring moment. Here's what that number actually means, how it happens, and the exact steps to take next.

How a password ends up "breached"

A breach doesn't mean someone hacked you personally. It means a company you had an account with — a shopping site, a forum, an old app you forgot about — got hacked, and their user database (including passwords) leaked publicly or got sold. Security researchers and services collect these leaked lists so the passwords in them can be checked against.

Why "found 4,213 times" doesn't mean 4,213 people know your specific password

It means that exact password string has appeared 4,213 times across all the breach datasets combined — often because it's a common password thousands of different people happened to also choose (like "Password123!" or a pet's name plus a birth year). The count is a popularity signal, not a targeting signal.

Why this still matters

Attackers run these breach lists against login pages automatically — a technique called credential stuffing. If your password appears in a breach and you've reused it anywhere, that account is at real risk, regardless of who else also has the same password.

What to actually do if a password comes back breached

  • Change it immediately on every account where you used it — not just one
  • Never reuse passwords across accounts — one leak shouldn't be able to unlock everything else
  • Use a password manager so each account gets a unique, random password you don't have to remember
  • Turn on two-factor authentication wherever it's offered — this protects you even if a password does leak later

Does checking my password here send it anywhere?

No — our checker uses a technique called k-anonymity. Your password is hashed inside your browser, and only the first 5 characters of that hash are sent to check against. The full password never leaves your device. This is the same method the industry-standard Have I Been Pwned service uses.
