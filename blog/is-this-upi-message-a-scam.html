Is This UPI/SMS Message a Scam? 8 Warning Signs

If you've gotten a text about a "pending delivery," a "KYC update," or an unexpected UPI payment request, you're not alone — these are some of the most common fraud patterns targeting phones in India right now. Here's what to actually check before you tap anything.

1. It asks you to pay a small "fee" to receive something

Real couriers don't collect customs or delivery fees via a personal payment link sent by SMS.

"Your parcel is on hold at customs. Pay Rs.15 to release: bit.ly/xyz123abc"

2. It creates urgency

Scam messages are built to make you act before you think. "Within 24 hours," "immediately," "account will be blocked today" — these phrases exist to short-circuit your normal caution.

3. It uses a shortened or unfamiliar link

Legitimate banks and government services link to their own domain, not a bit.ly or tinyurl. A shortened link hides the real destination until you've already clicked.

4. It asks for your OTP, PIN, or CVV

No bank, delivery service, or government body will ever call or text asking for your OTP. This is the single most reliable tell — if a message or caller asks for a one-time code, it's a scam, full stop.

5. It threatens your account will be blocked

"Your bank account has been suspended due to KYC non-compliance. Update immediately: [link]"

Banks handle KYC issues through their app or branch, not through a text with a link demanding immediate action.

6. Generic greeting instead of your name

"Dear Customer" or "Dear User" instead of your actual name is common in mass-sent scam blasts — legitimate transactional messages from your bank usually reference your name or account details.

7. Too-good-to-be-true prizes or cashback

You didn't enter a lucky draw you don't remember. Unexpected "you've won" messages exist to get you excited enough to skip verification.

8. It asks you to install a remote-access app

Being asked to install AnyDesk, TeamViewer, or "screen share" to "fix" an issue is one of the most damaging patterns — it gives a scammer live control of your phone, including anything you type.

What to do if you already clicked

  • Don't enter any information on the page that opens — close it immediately
  • If you entered an OTP or PIN, contact your bank's official helpline (from the number on your card, not the message) right away
  • Report the number to your carrier and to cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930 (India's cybercrime helpline)
